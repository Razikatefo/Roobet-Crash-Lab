## 🧭 Overview

**Momentum Lens** is a statistical observation studio built for people who enjoy studying demo rounds of multiplier-style games. Instead of chasing a mythical shortcut, Momentum Lens gives you a calm, glass-walled observatory where hundreds of simulated rounds can be replayed, compared, annotated, and turned into readable trends.

Think of it as a telescope for randomness — you are not bending the stars, you are learning how to watch them move.

The project began as an experiment: run a few hundred rounds in demo mode, compare the outcomes, and follow a simulation pipeline that highlights clusters, gaps, streaks, and cooldowns. Over time, the experiment grew into a full toolkit with a responsive interface, multilingual labels, and a community of curious analysts who simply love patterns.

> Momentum Lens does not predict the future of any live game. It studies demo behavior, and the joy is in the studying.

## 🏗️ Architecture at a Glance

Momentum Lens is layered like a quiet library:

- **Ingestion Layer** — collects demo round data from local simulation files.
- **Normalization Layer** — transforms every round into a consistent record.
- **Analysis Layer** — computes streaks, gaps, thresholds, and cluster density.
- **Visualization Layer** — renders charts and timelines with accessible colors.
- **Interaction Layer** — handles filters, annotations, and comparisons.
- **Localization Layer** — swaps interface strings across supported locales.

Each layer can be swapped independently, which keeps the project adaptable as new ideas arrive.

---

## 🧪 How the Simulation Pipeline Works

1. **Seed a session** — choose a number of demo rounds.
2. **Run the simulation** — the engine records each round locally.
3. **Compare sessions** — place two or more sessions in the comparator.
4. **Annotate observations** — mark interesting moments with personal notes.
5. **Export a summary** — keep a portable record of your study.

The pipeline is intentionally transparent. Every intermediate value can be inspected, which means you never have to trust a black box.
